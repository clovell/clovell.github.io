Latin Participle and Infinitive Formation Guide

A comprehensive reference for deriving verbal nouns and adjectives.

Determining Conjugation

Before forming any participle or infinitive, you must first determine the conjugation to which a verb belongs. This is done by examining the ending of the second principal part (the present active infinitive). If the second principal part ends in -āre, the verb belongs to the 1st conjugation. If it ends in -ēre, it belongs to the 2nd conjugation. If it ends in -ere, it belongs to the 3rd conjugation (note that if the first principal part ends in -iō alongside an -ere infinitive, it is a 3rd-io verb). Finally, if it ends in -īre, it belongs to the 4th conjugation. Knowing the conjugation dictates which vowels will connect the stem to the suffix.

Conjugation 1st Principal Part Ending 2nd Principal Part Ending Example
1st -ō -āre amō, amāre
2nd -eō -ēre moneō, monēre
3rd -ō -ere regō, regere
3rd-io -iō -ere capiō, capere
4th -iō -īre audiō, audīre

The Participles

Present Active Participle

To form the present active participle, you must locate the present stem of the verb by dropping the -re from the second principal part. For 1st, 2nd, and regular 3rd conjugation verbs, simply add the endings -ns (for the nominative singular) and -ntis (for the genitive singular). For example, amāre becomes amāns, amantis. However, if the verb is 3rd-io or 4th conjugation, you must introduce an -iē- before the consonant cluster, resulting in forms like capiēns, capientis or audiēns, audientis.

Conjugation Stem Preparation Suffix (Nom / Gen) Example Form
1st Drop -re -ns / -ntis amāns, amantis
2nd Drop -re -ns / -ntis monēns, monentis
3rd Drop -re -ns / -ntis regēns, regentis
3rd-io Drop -re + add -iē- -ns / -ntis capiēns, capientis
4th Drop -re + add -iē- -ns / -ntis audiēns, audientis

Perfect Passive Participle

The perfect passive participle requires no active morphological construction; it is simply the fourth principal part of the verb. It functions as a standard 1st and 2nd declension adjective and is listed in the dictionary as ending in -us, -a, -um. For example, the fourth principal part of videō is vīsus, making the participle vīsus, -a, -um.

Future Active Participle

To form the future active participle, you must extract the participial stem from the fourth principal part. Do this by removing the -us ending from the perfect passive participle. To this stem, append the future active suffix -ūrus, -a, -um. For example, taking portātus, removing the -us, and adding the suffix yields portātūrus, -a, -um. This form is often translated as "about to [verb]."

The Infinitives

Present Active Infinitive

The present active infinitive is the most straightforward form in the paradigm: it is identical to the second principal part of the verb as listed in the dictionary entry. For example, the present active infinitive of currō is simply currere.

Present Passive Infinitive

The formation of the present passive infinitive depends heavily on the conjugation. For verbs of the 1st, 2nd, and 4th conjugations, you simply change the final -e of the second principal part to an -ī (e.g., laudāre becomes laudārī, habēre becomes habērī, audīre becomes audīrī). However, for 3rd and 3rd-io conjugation verbs, you must drop the entire -ere ending from the second principal part and replace it with an -ī (e.g., dūcere becomes dūcī, facere becomes fierī [an irregular exception], capere becomes capī).

Perfect Active Infinitive

To form the perfect active infinitive, you must locate the perfect active stem by removing the -ī ending from the third principal part. To this stem, append the suffix -isse. For instance, the third principal part of mittō is mīsī. Dropping the -ī yields the stem mīs-, to which we add the suffix to form mīsisse.

Perfect Passive Infinitive

The perfect passive infinitive is a compound form. It consists of the perfect passive participle (the fourth principal part, ending in -us, -a, -um) paired with the present active infinitive of the verb sum, which is esse. For example, doctus, -a, -um esse.

Future Active Infinitive

Similarly, the future active infinitive is also a compound form. It pairs the future active participle (derived from the fourth principal part + -ūrus, -a, -um) with the present active infinitive esse. For example, monitūrus, -a, -um esse.

Future Passive Infinitive

The future passive infinitive is relatively rare but follows a strict formula. It pairs the neuter singular form of the perfect passive participle (technically the supine, ending in -um) with the present passive infinitive of the verb eō, which is īrī. Because the first element is the supine rather than a true participle, it does not decline to agree with a subject. An example is amatum īrī.
