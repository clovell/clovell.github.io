Perfect Passive System & 21 Target Verbs

Reference guide for forms, Easy vs. Hard translation modes, and principal parts

1. How the Perfect Passive System Works (60% / 20% / 20% Distribution)

In Latin, all three tenses of the Perfect Passive System are two-word periphrastic forms built from the 4th Principal Part (the Perfect Passive Participle) plus a form of sum, esse. The participle agrees with the subject in Gender and Number (Nominative: -us, -a, -um in the singular; -ī, -ae, -a in the plural).

Perfect Passive 60% freq
4th PP + sum, es, est, sumus, estis, sunt
English has two equivalents: was / were [verb]ed AND has / have been [verb]ed
Pluperfect Passive 20% freq
4th PP + eram, erās, erat, erāmus, erātis, erant
English: had been [verb]ed
Future Perfect Passive 20% freq
4th PP + erō, eris, erit, erimus, eritis, erunt
English: will have been [verb]ed

2. Easy Mode vs. Hard Mode (Step 2 Translation Quiz)

Quiz: Easy Mode (Passive Tense Focus)

All 4 choices are passive and use the correct subject pronoun and verb meaning: "I was ordered", "I have been ordered", "I had been ordered", and "I will have been ordered". When the verb is Perfect Passive, you must choose both correct English translations!

Quiz: Hard Mode (Full Morphology & Voice)

Tests active vs. passive voice (e.g., "I ordered", "I had ordered"), subject pronoun & gender agreement (he / she / it / they), and present/imperfect/future passive distractors ("I am being ordered", "I will be ordered").
